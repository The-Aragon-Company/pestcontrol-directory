If this is your first time booking a pest control appointment, a little advance knowledge makes the whole thing less stressful. Here's what actually happens, step by step.

How long does a first pest control visit take?

For a standard general pest treatment (ants, roaches, spiders, seasonal bugs), plan on 30 to 60 minutes. The technician will walk the interior and exterior, look for entry points and nesting spots, and apply treatment to baseboards, entry points, and problem areas.

Specialty visits run longer. A termite inspection can take 45–90 minutes depending on home size and accessibility (crawl space, attic, basement). A bed bug inspection or initial treatment can take 1–3 hours because it involves detailed room-by-room checking of mattresses, furniture seams, and baseboards. Ask when you book so you can plan your schedule.

Is pest control safe for kids and pets?

Licensed technicians use EPA-registered products applied according to label directions, and label directions include safety measures like re-entry times. As a general rule, most treated areas are safe to walk on and touch once the product has dried — often within one to two hours — but always ask your technician for the specific re-entry time for the products used at your home.

A few sensible precautions for the day of service:

  • Keep kids and pets out of the immediate treatment area while the technician is working.
  • Ask about fish tanks and reptile enclosures specifically — these often need to be covered or have air pumps turned off temporarily, since some pest products are harmful to aquatic life.
  • If anyone in the home is pregnant, has respiratory issues, or has chemical sensitivities, mention it before the visit so the technician can adjust the approach.

This is general guidance, not a medical recommendation — for specific health concerns, check with your doctor and ask the technician directly about the products being used that day.

Do you need to leave the house during treatment?

For most general pest control visits, no — you can stay home and go about your day, though you'll likely be asked to step out of the room being treated for a few minutes at a time. For more intensive jobs, you may need to leave for a set period:

  • General pest control: stay home, avoid the treated area briefly.
  • Bed bug heat treatment: typically requires leaving the home for several hours while the whole space is heated.
  • Fumigation (whole-structure): requires vacating the home for one to several days, with a clearance inspection before you re-enter. This is rare for typical residential pest issues and is mainly used for severe termite or bed bug cases.
  • Termite liquid or bait treatment: usually doesn't require leaving the home.

Ask specifically: "Do I need to be out of the house for this, and if so, for how long?" A good company will tell you this when you book, not when the technician arrives.

How should you prepare for your first visit?

A little prep helps the technician do a more thorough job and speeds things up:

  1. Clear access to baseboards and corners. Move furniture, boxes, and clutter away from walls where possible.
  2. Clear kitchen counters and under-sink cabinets. These are common treatment spots.
  3. Put away pet food and water bowls during the visit, and secure pets in a room not being treated.
  4. Note problem areas in advance. Write down where you've seen pests, so you can point the technician there directly.
  5. Cover fish tanks and turn off air pumps if instructed by the technician.
  6. Plan for re-entry time. Know roughly when it will be safe for kids and pets back in treated rooms.
  7. If you rent, get your landlord or property manager involved first. They usually have to approve treatment before a technician can apply anything.

Who has to approve the treatment?

If you own the home you live in, this one's simple. If you rent, it's the thing most likely to slow your appointment down.

Across 7,799 calls where we could see how things turned out, calls from someone living in a home they own ended with an appointment 36.6% of the time (2,325 calls), compared with 23.8% for renters (567 calls). And callers who confirmed they were the homeowner were roughly three times more likely to get an appointment scheduled (36.0%) than callers where ownership never came up at all (11.6%).

That isn't a judgment about renters. It's a practical reality: a technician generally can't treat a rental without the owner's or manager's sign-off, so if nobody on the call can authorize the work, it stalls.

If you rent, the fastest path looks like this:

  • Tell your landlord or property manager what you're seeing, in writing — a text or email creates a record.
  • Ask who's booking it. Many leases make pest control the landlord's responsibility, and some already have a company they use.
  • If they want you to schedule it, get their okay to authorize treatment plus a phone number the company can call.
  • Have that ready when you book, so the conversation doesn't end with "we'll need to talk to the owner first."

What happens after the technician leaves?

You'll typically get a summary of what was found, what was treated, and what to expect next — including how long it takes to see results and whether you need a follow-up visit. Most general pest issues take a few days to a week to fully die down since the goal is often to eliminate the colony, not just the bugs you can see.

If pests are still active after the timeframe the technician gave you, call the company back — most plans include a free re-treatment window. See pest control contracts explained for how these guarantees typically work.

When to call a professional

If you're seeing pests daily, finding them in multiple rooms, spotting signs of termites (mud tubes, hollow-sounding wood, discarded wings), or dealing with bed bugs or rodents, don't wait — these situations tend to get worse and more expensive the longer they go untreated. A first visit from a licensed pro gets you an accurate read on the problem and a real treatment plan instead of guesswork.

FAQ

How long does a first pest control visit usually take?

Most general pest control visits take 30 to 60 minutes. Specialty visits — termite inspections, bed bug treatments, or larger homes — can take longer, sometimes up to a few hours, so it's worth asking your specific timeframe when you book.

Is it safe to stay home during pest control treatment?

Yes, for most standard treatments you can stay home, though you may be asked to leave the treated room briefly. More intensive treatments like whole-home fumigation or bed bug heat treatment may require leaving the house for a set period — ask ahead of time.

When can kids and pets go back into a treated room?

This depends on the specific product used, but it's often one to two hours once treatments have dried. Always ask your technician for the exact re-entry time for the products applied in your home that day.

Do I need to clean before the pest control technician arrives?

You don't need to deep clean, but clearing access to baseboards, corners, and under-sink cabinets helps the technician treat problem areas more thoroughly. A quick declutter of the areas where you've seen pests is the most useful prep step.

I rent — can I book pest control myself?

Sometimes, but check with your landlord or property manager first, since they typically have to authorize treatment on a property they own. In 7,799 calls where we could see the outcome, renters got an appointment scheduled 23.8% of the time versus 36.6% for people living in a home they own — usually a sign-off problem, not a service problem. Many leases also make pest control the landlord's responsibility, so raising it with them first can save you both time and money.

What if I still see bugs after the first treatment?

It's common to see some pests for several days after treatment as the product works through the colony. If pests are still active well beyond the timeframe your technician gave you, call the company back — most plans include a free follow-up visit.
