Paying for pest control doesn't guarantee a pest-free home by itself — how you prep, what you do during treatment, and whether you follow up all affect the outcome. Here's how to get your money's worth from a professional visit.

Why does prep matter so much?

Pesticides and baits work by contact or ingestion, so anything blocking access to a treated area reduces effectiveness. If cabinets are packed with dishes, a technician can't treat behind them. If a mattress and box spring aren't stripped for a bed bug treatment, hidden bugs survive. Prep isn't busywork — it's the difference between a treatment that works and one that only handles the pests that happened to be out in the open that day.

What should you do before the technician arrives?

Your pest control company should give you specific prep instructions based on the pest and treatment type, but common steps include:

  1. Clear out from under sinks and inside cabinets in kitchens and bathrooms.
  2. Vacuum floors, especially along baseboards, before certain treatments (this removes food debris that competes with bait).
  3. Move furniture away from walls a few inches so technicians can reach baseboards.
  4. Store food, dishes, and utensils in sealed containers or a different room.
  5. Remove pet food, water bowls, and toys from treatment areas, and secure pets elsewhere.
  6. For bed bug treatments specifically, strip and wash bedding on high heat and reduce clutter around the bed.
  7. Note every room or area where you've seen activity so you can point the technician there directly.

One prep step almost nobody thinks about: make sure whoever owns the property can approve the work. This shows up clearly in our own data. Across 7,799 calls where we could see the outcome — part of 18,941 calls from homeowners to pest control companies across 13 companies between January and July 2026 — callers who confirmed they owned the home ended up with an appointment scheduled 36.0% of the time. When ownership never came up during the call at all, that fell to 11.6%. Confirmed owners were about three times more likely to walk away with a booked appointment, and a third of callers were never even asked (2,613 of 7,799).

The practical version: if you own the home, say so early. If you rent, talk to your landlord or property manager before you book, since many handle pest control directly. And if you're arranging service for a parent, a co-owned property, or a rental you own, have the person who can approve and pay for the work on the call or reachable while you're on it. Treatments get held up over a signature far more often than over scheduling.

What should you do during the visit?

Be available to answer questions and show the technician exactly where you've seen pests — this saves time and helps them target the treatment accurately. Ask any of the questions from our 15 questions to ask before hiring a pest control company guide if you haven't already, especially around reentry times and what to expect next.

Once treatment starts, stay out of the areas being treated until the technician tells you it's safe to return. Keep kids and pets out of those rooms for the full recommended window, even if it looks and smells fine sooner. If you have specific health sensitivities, mention them before treatment begins, not after.

What should you do right after treatment?

Avoid immediately cleaning treated surfaces — mopping floors or wiping down baseboards right after treatment can remove the product before it has a chance to work. Ask the technician how long to wait before your normal cleaning routine resumes in treated areas.

Keep an eye out over the following days and note any continued activity, since this information matters for your follow-up visit. Don't panic if you still see a few pests in the first day or two; many treatments take time to fully take effect as pests contact the product and die off gradually.

Why do follow-up visits matter?

Many pest problems, especially termites, bed bugs, and rodents, aren't solved in a single visit by nature. Termite treatments often require monitoring over months. Bed bugs frequently need two or more treatments spaced a couple of weeks apart to catch newly hatched eggs. Rodent work usually includes a follow-up to check traps and seal any entry points missed the first time.

Skipping a scheduled follow-up is one of the most common reasons homeowners feel like "the treatment didn't work" when in reality the job was only half finished. If you're not sure what your treatment plan requires, ask directly: how many visits total, and what's checked at each one.

When to call a professional

Some pest situations are well-suited to DIY: an occasional ant on the counter, a single spider, or a wasp that wandered in through an open door. But you should call a licensed professional when you're facing a true infestation rather than an isolated sighting, structural pests like termites, health-risk pests like bed bugs, rodents, or aggressive stinging insects, or a problem that keeps returning despite your own efforts. DIY treatments applied incorrectly can also make some infestations worse by scattering a colony instead of eliminating it.

Frequently asked questions

How long after pest control treatment can I clean my house?

This depends on the product and area treated, so always ask your technician directly. As a general rule, avoid mopping or wiping treated baseboards and surfaces for at least a few days so the product has time to work, but normal cleaning in untreated areas is usually fine right away.

Why am I still seeing bugs after pest control treatment?

Some die-off happens gradually over days as pests contact the treatment, so seeing a few after the visit isn't unusual. If activity hasn't noticeably decreased after the window your technician gave you, or if it's increasing, call the company back to report it.

Do I need to leave the house during pest control treatment?

Usually not for standard treatments, though you may need to stay out of specific treated rooms for a set time. Some treatments, like certain fumigation methods for severe infestations, do require leaving the property; your technician will tell you if that applies to your situation.

How many pest control visits does it usually take to solve a problem?

It depends heavily on the pest. A general ant or roach issue might resolve in one or two visits, while termites, bed bugs, and some rodent problems commonly require a series of visits spaced over weeks or months.

Is it my fault if pests come back after treatment?

Not necessarily — some pests are naturally harder to eliminate in one round, and outdoor conditions can reintroduce pests regardless of what you do inside. That said, skipping prep steps or follow-up visits does reduce effectiveness, so following instructions closely gives you the best odds.

Should I keep doing my own DIY treatments alongside professional service?

Ask your technician first. Some DIY sprays and repellents can interfere with professional-grade products or make pests avoid a treated area instead of contacting it, which can actually slow things down.
