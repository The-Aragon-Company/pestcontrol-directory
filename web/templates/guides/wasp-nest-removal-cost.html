What factors affect wasp nest removal cost?

Nest removal pricing depends on a handful of practical things a technician has to account for before they ever pick up a sprayer.

  1. Nest location. A nest hanging from a low tree branch or under an eave is quick and cheap to treat. A nest inside a wall void, attic, chimney, or high up on a two-story roofline requires ladders, protective gear, and more time, which raises the price.
  2. Nest size and colony size. A small, new nest with a handful of wasps costs less to treat than a large, established nest with hundreds of insects, which may need multiple treatment passes.
  3. Type of stinging insect. Paper wasps and yellow jackets are usually more affordable to treat than aggressive species like hornets, and underground yellow jacket nests can require extra care since they're harder to locate precisely.
  4. Number of nests. If you have multiple nests on the property, expect a per-nest fee or a discounted bundled rate rather than a single flat price.
  5. Accessibility and safety equipment needed. Nests requiring a ladder, full protective suit, or removal of siding/soffit to reach a wall void will cost more due to the added time and risk.
  6. Follow-up visit needs. Some nests (especially large or established ones) need a second visit to confirm all activity has stopped, which may or may not be included in the original quote.

Published industry ranges put wasp nest removal at $300–$700 for most homeowners, with an average around $525. A small, easy nest can be as low as $100, while a large or hard-to-reach one can run up to $1,300. By species, paper wasp nests typically run about $100–$200 and hornet nests about $300–$400, while yellowjackets run $500–$1,200 when the nest is concealed underground or inside a wall. Your price still depends on your region, the property, and how big the colony is, so it's worth getting a quote rather than guessing.

How much does nest removal cost by location and type?

Exterior nest, easily accessible (eaves, low branches, porch): Typically $100–$250. This is the most common and quickest job — the technician treats the nest directly and removes it in one visit.

Nest inside a wall void, attic, or chimney: Often $300–$600+, since reaching the nest may require opening drywall, accessing the attic, or using specialized equipment to treat without fully exposing the nest.

High or hard-to-reach nests (rooflines, tall trees): Usually $200–$450, reflecting the added time and ladder or lift equipment needed to safely reach the nest.

Underground yellow jacket nests: Typically $150–$350, since locating the exact nest entrance and treating it safely takes more care than a visible hanging nest.

Beehive removal (as opposed to wasp/hornet): Often priced differently, since many companies use live-removal and relocation methods rather than extermination. Published industry ranges put beehive removal at $135–$1,065, with most homeowners paying around $445. Live relocation of honeybees or bumble bees typically runs $125–$1,000 depending on colony size, while bee extermination runs $100–$800. Honey bee removal has the widest published spread at $75–$2,000; carpenter and bumble bee jobs usually run $75–$500. Worth knowing: some local beekeepers will relocate a honeybee hive or swarm for free to stock their own hives, so it's worth a call before you pay for removal.

What's included at each price level?

Budget-tier ($100–$200): Treatment of a single, easily accessible exterior nest with straightforward spray or dust application. Usually a one-visit job with no follow-up included.

Mid-tier ($200–$400): Nest removal in a moderately difficult location (higher up, inside a small void, or a larger nest), often including nest removal/cleanup after treatment and a short follow-up guarantee if activity continues.

Premium-tier ($400–$600+): Complex jobs — nests deep inside a wall or attic, multiple nests on one property, or aggressive species requiring extra protective equipment. Usually includes a callback guarantee and sometimes minor repair or resealing of the access point used to reach the nest.

Is it worth paying a pro to remove a wasp nest?

For most nests, yes. Wasps and hornets can sting repeatedly (unlike honeybees), and disturbing a nest without the right protective gear and timing is one of the more common ways people get seriously stung. A professional treats the nest at the right time of day (when insects are less active) and with the right protective equipment, reducing risk significantly.

DIY store-bought sprays can work for very small, low, easily reached nests, but they often only kill the visible insects while leaving the queen and nest structure intact — meaning the colony can rebuild in the same spot. When to call a professional

Call a pro immediately if the nest is inside a wall, attic, or chimney, if anyone in the household has a known allergy to stings, if the nest is large or has been there for a while (meaning a bigger colony), or if it's hornets or yellow jackets rather than a small paper wasp nest. These situations carry real risk of multiple stings if disturbed incorrectly.

You should also call a pro if a nest keeps rebuilding in the same spot after a DIY attempt — that usually means the queen wasn't eliminated. Trying to save money by knocking down or spraying a nest yourself, especially at the wrong time of day, is one of the more common ways people end up in urgent care. If you're not sure what kind of stinging insect you're dealing with, a quick pest inspection can identify it and recommend the safest removal approach.

FAQ

How much does it cost to remove a wasp nest from a house?

Most homeowners pay $100–$500, depending on where the nest is located. Easy-to-reach exterior nests are on the lower end, while nests in walls, attics, or high rooflines cost more due to added labor and equipment.

Is hornet nest removal more expensive than wasp nest removal?

It can be, since hornets are more aggressive and often require extra protective equipment and more careful timing. The bigger cost driver, though, is usually nest location rather than species alone.

Can I remove a wasp nest myself to save money?

For a small, low, easily reached nest, some homeowners do it themselves with store-bought sprays, though this carries real sting risk. For nests in walls, attics, or high locations, or for larger colonies, professional removal is strongly recommended.

Why do nests inside walls cost more to remove?

Treating a nest inside a wall void or attic often requires locating the exact spot, sometimes opening a small access point, and using specialized application methods to reach insects a surface spray can't. This added time and equipment increases the cost.

Will the wasps come back after removal?

If the nest and queen are fully treated, wasps generally won't rebuild in that exact spot, though new wasps may build a nest elsewhere on the property in future seasons. Many companies offer a short-term guarantee if activity continues after treatment.

Is bee removal priced the same as wasp removal?

Not usually. Because honeybees are important pollinators, many companies use live-removal and relocation services for bees rather than extermination, which can be priced differently than standard wasp or hornet treatment. Published ranges put beehive removal at $135–$1,065, with most homeowners paying around $445, versus about $300–$700 for a typical wasp nest. Some local beekeepers will even relocate a honeybee swarm for free, so ask before you pay.
