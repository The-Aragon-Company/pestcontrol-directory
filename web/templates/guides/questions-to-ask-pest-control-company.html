Hiring the wrong pest control company can mean wasted money, a problem that never actually gets solved, or worse, unlicensed work with no recourse if something goes wrong. These 15 questions cover the essentials — licensing, insurance, guarantees, chemicals, and pricing — so you walk into the conversation prepared.

Ask this before all 15: do you handle my pest?

Before you get into licensing or pricing, make sure you're even talking to the right kind of company. Across 18,941 calls from homeowners to pest control companies between January and July 2026, spanning 13 pest control companies, general bug work was widely available: only 3.8% of cockroach calls and 3.8% of rodent calls reached a company that doesn't do that work. Specialty trades were much spottier. Wildlife calls landed at a company that doesn't do wildlife work 19.3% of the time, wasp and hornet calls 11.2%, termite calls 10.6%, and bed bug calls 10.3%.

So if you're dealing with bats, termites, bed bugs, or a hornet nest, open with it: "Do you handle bats?" It takes ten seconds and saves you from describing your whole problem to someone who was never going to be able to help.

Is the company properly licensed?

1. What's your state pest control license number?

Every state requires pest control operators to be licensed, and legitimate companies will give you this number without hesitation. You can usually verify it through your state's department of agriculture or pesticide regulatory office. If a company dodges this question or claims licensing "doesn't apply" to them, walk away.

2. Are the technicians who show up licensed or certified, or just the company?

In many states, both the business and the individual applicator need certification. Ask specifically whether the technician coming to your home is licensed, not just the office you're calling.

Is the company insured?

3. Do you carry general liability and workers' compensation insurance?

This protects you if a technician is injured on your property or if something gets damaged during treatment. Ask for proof of insurance, not just a verbal yes.

4. What happens if my property is damaged during treatment?

A legitimate company should have a clear process for handling damage claims. Get this in writing before treatment begins, not after something goes wrong.

What does the guarantee actually cover?

5. What's covered under your service guarantee?

Guarantees vary widely. Some cover free re-treatments if pests return within a set window; others are limited or don't apply to certain pests. Get the specifics — not just "we guarantee our work."

6. How long does the guarantee last, and what voids it?

Ask about the time window and any conditions that could void coverage, like skipping a recommended follow-up or not addressing a moisture problem the inspector flagged.

7. What happens if the pest control doesn't work the first time?

Find out whether re-treatment is free, discounted, or billed as a new service. This is one of the most important questions since recurring problems are common with certain pests.

What chemicals and methods will they use?

8. What products do you plan to use, and are they EPA-registered?

You don't need a chemistry lesson, but you deserve to know generally what's being applied in your home, especially if you have kids, pets, or health sensitivities. A trustworthy technician can explain this in plain language.

9. Is the treatment safe for my pets and kids, and when can we go back into treated areas?

Ask for specific reentry times for each treated area. This is basic safety information any company should provide clearly, and it's worth writing down.

10. Do you offer non-chemical or lower-impact treatment options?

Many companies offer options like baiting, traps, or exclusion work (sealing entry points) that reduce chemical use. Ask what's available for your specific situation.

How does pricing work?

11. Is this a one-time treatment or does it require a contract?

Get clear on whether you're paying for a single visit or signing up for recurring service. Read our guide on pest control contracts explained before signing anything long-term.

12. What's included in the quoted price, and what could cost extra?

Ask about follow-up visits, additional treatments for a worsening problem, or travel fees. A detailed, itemized quote is a good sign; a vague flat number is not.

13. Can I get the quote in writing before you start?

Never agree to work based on a verbal price alone. A written quote protects you if there's a dispute later.

What should you ask about the process itself?

14. What should I do to prepare my home before treatment, and what should I do afterward?

Good technicians will walk you through prep steps and after-care instructions, since your follow-through affects how well the treatment works. See our guide on how to get the best results from professional pest control for what that typically involves.

15. How many visits will this take, and what happens at each one?

Some pests, like termites or bed bugs, need multiple visits by nature. Understand the full timeline up front rather than being surprised by follow-up costs later.

One thing companies will ask you: whether you own the property. It's not nosiness — nobody can treat, seal, or drill into a home without the owner's approval. In the calls we looked at, confirmed homeowners ended up with an appointment 36.0% of the time. When the caller wasn't the owner, that dropped to 17.8%. So if you rent, loop in your landlord or property manager first, and if you're calling on behalf of a parent or a rental you own with someone else, be ready to say who signs off on the work.

When to call a professional

If you've tried store-bought sprays or traps and the problem keeps coming back, or you're dealing with termites, bed bugs, rodents, or a stinging insect nest, it's time to bring in a licensed pro. These situations tend to involve infestations rather than isolated pests, and mishandling them — especially termites or bed bugs — can make the problem worse or spread it further.

Frequently asked questions

How do I verify a pest control company's license?

Most states have an online lookup tool through their department of agriculture, pesticide regulatory agency, or professional licensing board. Search for your state name plus "pest control license lookup," and enter the license number the company gave you to confirm it's active and matches their business name.

What's a reasonable guarantee period for pest control?

This varies by pest and company, and there's no single industry standard, so it's worth asking directly rather than assuming. Get the guarantee terms in writing, including exactly which pests and situations are covered.

Should I be worried if a company won't put their quote in writing?

Yes. A written quote is standard practice and protects both you and the company. If a technician resists giving you anything on paper, treat that as a signal to get a second quote elsewhere.

Do I need to sign a long-term contract for pest control?

Not necessarily. Many pest problems can be handled with a one-time treatment, while others, like ongoing rodent or ant prevention, benefit from a recurring service plan. Ask about both options and choose based on your actual situation, not sales pressure.

Is it normal for a technician not to know exactly what chemical they're using?

No. A trained technician should be able to tell you the general product type and whether it's EPA-registered, even if they don't have the exact formulation memorized. If they can't answer basic safety questions, that's worth flagging.

What if I ask these questions and the company gets defensive?

That reaction alone is useful information. A professional, licensed company is used to answering these questions and shouldn't be bothered by a homeowner doing due diligence.
